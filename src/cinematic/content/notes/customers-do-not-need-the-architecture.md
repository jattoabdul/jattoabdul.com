While reviewing RuleNorth, I found screens that explained more about the implementation than about what the merchant needed to do. The information made sense from inside the project. That did not automatically make it useful from inside a shop.

The work became more concrete when we brought the interface back to everyday questions. Is the connection healthy? Does anything need my attention? Can I change this setting and know that it saved?

That last question mattered. Making a screen clearer was not only a copy exercise. The settings needed to behave like the controls they appeared to be, including when saving failed or information changed.

I am learning to look at the interface from the other side of the work. The person using it did not sit through the architecture discussions. They should not have to reconstruct them to take the next step.

Understanding a system deeply is part of my job. Making someone else carry all that understanding is not. Sometimes the better product comes from deciding what the customer no longer needs to think about.
